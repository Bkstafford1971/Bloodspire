Welcome to the Bloodspire Arena

BLOODSPIRE ARENA is an arena combat game where you manage a team of five warriors and guide them through increasingly difficult fights. Each warrior has six core attributes, learns skills over time, and can be injured. The system is deeply mechanical but rewards learning its systems.

Core Concepts

The Game Loop:

  1. Create and equip your team of 5 warriors
  2. Select fights (or let matchmaking choose)
  3. Set up strategies before each fight (weapon load-out, combat style, triggers)
  4. Watch the fight unfold in real-time
  5. Collect rewards, train skills and attributes, manage injuries

The Six Attributes: All warriors have six core attributes ranging from 3 (worst) to 25 (best):

  • Strength (STR): Determines damage output and maximum weapon weight you can wield
  • Dexterity (DEX): Affects attack and defense accuracy, dodge chance, initiative
  • Constitution (CON): Determines maximum HP and endurance; crucial for survival
  • Intelligence (INT): Improves skill training speed; affects strategy
  • Presence (PRE): Determines charisma in the arena; affects popularity and concede chances
  • Size (SIZ): Cannot be trained; affects physical measurements and melee interactions

Wisdom: During warrior creation, you get 16 points to distribute among the six base stats (max 7 per stat). Choose wisely, attribute training later is slow.

Fight Outcomes

Fights are decided by:

  • Wins = victories (warrior still alive)
  • Losses = defeats (warrior knocked unconscious or opponent survives)
  • Kills = wins where the opponent died

Retirement

Warriors can retire after 50 fights if the player requests it. Retired warriors leave your team permanently and are replaced.

Core Game Loop

Creating a Warrior

Each warrior starts with six randomly-generated base stats that sum to 55 (each stat 3–21). You then distribute 16 additional points (max 7 per stat, respecting the 25-point ceiling) to refine their attributes.

Key Decision: Different races favor different stats. Halflings want Dexterity, Half-Orcs want Strength, Dwarves want Constitution, etc. Choose your race first, then distribute accordingly.

Equipping a Warrior

Every warrior has two armor slots and three weapon slots:

Armor Slots:

  • Body Armor (0–8 tiers from Cloth to Full Plate)
  • Helm (0–5 tiers from Leather Cap to Full Helm)

Weapon Slots:

  • Primary Weapon (main fighting weapon)
  • Secondary Weapon (off-hand, often a shield or second blade)
  • Backup Weapon (carried but not used; can be swapped in during fights if you lose either your primary, or secondary weapon)

Strength Requirement: Your Strength limits the maximum weight of armor and weapons you can wear without penalty. Higher Strength allows heavier equipment.

Training

You may assign up to 3 training actions per turn to each warrior. Training can target:

  • Attributes: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence and Presence, but NOT Size.
  • Weapon Skills: one skill per weapon, scales 0–9.
  • Non-Weapon Skills: Dodge, Parry, Throw, Lunge, Disarm, Initiative, Feint, Brawl, Sweep, Cleave, Bash, Acrobatics, Riposte, Slash, Strike.

Training Success: Success depends on your Intelligence (for skills) or Constitution (for attributes). Higher stats = higher training success.

Training Speed: Humans and Gnomes train noticeably faster than other races. Later training (at higher levels) becomes progressively harder.

Permanent Injuries

When a warrior takes damage, there is a small chance they suffer a permanent injury in a specific body location:

  • Head, Chest, Abdomen, Primary Arm, Secondary Arm, Primary Leg, Secondary Leg

Each location can accumulate up to 9 levels of injury. Level 9 is fatal (warrior dies). Injuries cannot be healed; they are permanent and reduce combat effectiveness in that location.

Character Generation & Progressions

Initial parameters allocate random base values totaling exactly 55 points spread across the six major attributes. Managers apply their 16 discretionary points to optimize for unique racial archetypes. Selecting an initial structural race establishes the viable attribute framework.

Equipment Matrix

Gladiators utilize two specific defensive armor points alongside three active inventory slots:

Defensive Slots:

  • Body Armor (Scales from Tier 0 Cloth up to Tier 8 Full Plate).
  • Head Protection (Scales from Tier 0 Leather Cap up to Tier 5 Full Helm).

Weapon Implement Positions:

  • Primary Position (Active primary offensive implementation).
  • Secondary Position (Off-hand configurations, accepting dual weapons or shielding options).
  • Backup Position (Stowed backup implementation available for swap-out procedures after matches resolve).

Weight Thresholds: Wielding high-encumbrance heavy protective gear or heavy physical weaponry requires matching Strength metrics to prevent heavy execution velocity penalties.

Training Allocation

Managers assign up to 3 individual training tasks to every gladiator per cycle. Actions target core stats, individual weapon specializations (ranging from level 0 to 9), or distinct tactical maneuvers. Intelligence values determine the training velocity of complex combat skills, while Constitution scales attribute progress. Humans and Gnomes advance through training cycles at higher base rates.

Localized Anatomic Damage

Receiving incoming force calculations introduces small percentages for sustained localized permanent injuries across specific zones:

  • Anatomic Focus: Head, Chest, Abdomen, Primary Arm, Secondary Arm, Primary Leg, Secondary Leg.

Individual areas track distinct injury rankings from 1 to 9, accumulating persistent combat modifiers that cannot be removed. Reaching level 9 in any zone causes instant asset termination.

Racial Ancestries

Select a genetic profile from the dropdown panel to view structural analysis.

Human

Physical: 5'7" average (5'2"–6'4" range), 165 lbs

Lore: The adaptable everyman. No extreme strengths or weaknesses. Humans train faster and are less prone to injuries.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Accelerated Training – Humans learn skills and attributes faster than other races
  • Resilient – Humans suffer permanent injuries less often

Favorable Opponents: Most balanced race; effective against all opponents.

Unfavorable Opponents: None in particular.

Typical Build: Jack-of-all-trades. Short Sword with a Target Shield is a popular entry build. Good with many weapon types. Ideal for learning the game.

Half-Orc

Physical: 6'3" average (5'5"–7'6"), 259 lbs (heaviest race)

Lore: Pure brute force. Devastating damage and durability, but slow, clumsy, and easy to out-maneuver.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Brutal Strikes – Half-Orcs can deal significantly more damage per hit
  • Thick Hide – Higher maximum HP
  • Deliberate Swings – Typically attacks slower than other races
  • Lead Foot – Slower reactions can lead to reduced initiative
  • Poor Defenses – Below-average dodge and parry capability

Favorite Weapons: War Flail, Great Axe, Great Sword, War Hammer, Great Pick, Battle Flail, Maul, Halberd

Typical Build: Prioritize Strength and Constitution. War Flail or Great Axe are legendary in Half-Orc hands. Two-handed weapons reward high Strength severely. Defensive play is a death trap.

Halfling

Physical: 3'10" average (3'1"–5'1"), 49 lbs (lightest race)

Lore: Infuriatingly hard to hit. Fast and mobile but extremely fragile with light damage output.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Elusive – Exceptional dodge bonus; hardest to hit in the game
  • Lightning Fast – Very high attack rate
  • Martial Arts Affinity – Extra effectiveness with unarmed style
  • Lightweight – Rely on Dexterity and precise attacks for dealing damage
  • Poor Blocker – Below-average parry
  • Frail – Lower maximum HP

Favorite Weapons: Short Sword, Stiletto, Hatchet, Quarterstaff, Javelin, Bladed Flail, Hammer

Weak Weapons: Maul, Great Axe, Great Sword, Halberd (too heavy and slow)

Typical Build: Maximize Dexterity, keep Strength moderate. "Pocket Rocket" builds with a single Short Sword are iconic. Goal is to land many light hits before the opponent can land one heavy hit. Defensive play (Wall of Steel, Parry) doesn't suit them.

Dwarf

Physical: 4'2" average (3'6"–5'2"), 195 lbs (very dense, heavier than height suggests)

Lore: The ultimate tank. Absorbs massive punishment and parries masterfully, but very slow and poor at dodging.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Stout – Highest maximum HP in the game
  • Solid Blows – Above-average damage bonus
  • Master Parrier – Significant parry bonus
  • Sturdy Frame – Can wear heavier armor than Strength would normally allow
  • Shield Affinity – Bonus defense when using a shield (greater with their preferred shields)
  • Deliberate – Somewhat slower attack rate
  • Poor Dodger – Very poor dodge; should avoid light armor

Favorite Weapons: Battle Axe, Fransisca, Great Axe, Morningstar, War Hammer, Boar Spear, Target Shield, Net, Trident

Weak Weapons: Halberd, Pole Axe (doesn't suit the Dwarf style)

Typical Build: High Strength and Constitution, moderate Dexterity. Dwarves want armor and shields. Counterstrike and Parry are your friends. Target Shield + War Hammer is a classic. Strategy: Let the opponent come to you and parry relentlessly.

Half-Elf

Physical: 5'4" average (5'0"–6'0"), 144 lbs

Lore: Versatile and capable. Slight edge in weapon handling with no major weaknesses.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Weapon Aptitude – Counts as slightly stronger for weapon weight limits
  • Quick Hands – Slightly faster attack rate
  • Agile – Decent dodge bonus
  • Balanced – Mild all-rounder damage bonus

Favorite Weapons: Pole Axe, Bastard Sword, Long Sword, Scimitar, Battle Flail, Scythe, Javelin, Broadsword

Typical Build: Balanced Strength and Dexterity. Half-Elves can use slightly heavier weapons than their Strength would suggest. Battle Flail is a Half-Elf favorite. Its attack rate pairs well with their quickness. Good entry-level race after Humans.

Elf

Physical: 5'2" average (4'8"–5'11"), 129 lbs

Lore: Elusive speed demons. Masters of dual-wielding and evasion, but extremely fragile.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Graceful – High dodge bonus
  • Whirlwind – Very fast attack rate
  • Dual-Wielder – Bonus when both hands hold weapons (scales with weapon skill)
  • Delicate – Lower maximum HP
  • Light Hitter – Minor damage penalty

Favorite Weapons: Dagger, Short Sword, Scimitar, Scythe, Flail, Javelin, Stiletto, Epee

Typical Build: Maximize Dexterity, moderate Strength. "Wall of Steel + Dual Short Swords" is the classic Elf build. They can parry very frequently and take multiple light hits. Avoid heavy armor and heavy weapons; focus on high activity level and fast attacks.

Goblin

Physical: 3'6" average (3'2"–4'4"), 68 lbs (tiny, light)

Lore: Tiny dirty fighters. Fast and tricky with thrown weapons, but very weak and fragile.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Quick Hands – Very fast attack rate
  • First Strike – Acts first most of the time
  • Slippery – High dodge bonus
  • Thrown Mastery – Bonus to Opportunity Throw; thrown weapons are lighter for them
  • Scavenger – High chance to pick up dropped weapons
  • Too Small – Severe penalty with heavy or two-handed weapons
  • Weakling – Significant damage penalty
  • Brittle – Very low maximum HP and Strength

Favorite Weapons: Dagger, Stiletto, Short Sword, Hatchet, Javelin, Knife, Bola, Hammer

Weak Weapons: Great Axe, Great Sword, Halberd, War Flail (too heavy; suffer penalties)

Typical Build: Maximize Dexterity. Opportunity Throw specialist or fast hit-and-run. Light weapons only. The Scavenger ability lets you pick up your enemy's dropped weapons and turn them on them. High-risk, high-reward gameplay.

Gnome

Physical: 3'4" average (3'1"–4'2"), 60 lbs (smallest playable race)

Lore: Small, surprisingly tough tacticians. Excel at counterstrikes and turning aggression against opponents.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Surprisingly Sturdy – Decent maximum HP for their size
  • Quick Study – Slightly faster learning
  • Adept Blocker – Good parry bonus
  • Counterstrike Mastery – Strong bonus on successful parries and ripostes
  • Tactician's Edge – Bonus defense vs aggressive attackers, penalty vs methodical ones
  • Light Hitter – Minor damage penalty
  • Deliberate – Somewhat slower attack rate

Favorite Weapons: Short Sword, Long Sword, Epee, Bastard Sword, Hammer, Mace, Morningstar, War Hammer

Weak Weapons: Great Axe, Battle Axe, Halberd, Great Pick (too large)

Typical Build: Balanced Strength, Constitution, and Dexterity. Counterstrike specialist. Let the opponent attack you, then riposte viciously. The Tactician's Edge is subtle but powerful. Aggressive opponents find themselves at a disadvantage against you, so play patiently.

Lizardfolk

Physical: 6'0" average (5'8"–6'9"), 240 lbs (muscular, dense reptile)

Lore: Savage reptilian predators. Tough and relentless with natural armor, but cold-blooded and slow to accelerate.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Tough Scales – Good maximum HP and natural armor protection
  • Natural Weaponry – Bonus damage with Martial Combat or Open Hand
  • Surprisingly Agile – Decent dodge bonus for their size
  • Cold-Blooded – Slower attack rate (slow to start fighting)

Favorite Weapons: Open Hand, Dagger, Stiletto, Short Sword, Hatchet, Hammer, Mace, Quarterstaff

Weak Weapons: Epee, Short Sword, Long Sword (not suited to heavy fighting style)

Special Rules: Natural scales provide inherent defense. Light armor (Cloth, Leather) can add a small bonus, but heavier armor provides no additional protection while still imposing movement penalties. Recommended: No armor, Cloth, or Leather only. Their natural weapon attacks (Open Hand) can be devastating.

Typical Build: High Strength and Constitution. Martial Combat specialist with Open Hand. Their natural armor means they don't need heavy gear. Use mobility and natural weapon bonuses. Cold-blooded penalty is mitigated by patient, methodical play.

Tabaxi

Physical: 5'9" average (5'6"–6'4"), 165 lbs (lean feline)

Lore: Lightning-quick acrobatic felines. Best evasion in the game but fragile and tire quickly in long fights.

Mechanical Benefits:

  • Uncanny Dodge – Best pure evasion in the game
  • Lightning Reflexes – Acts first; determines combat flow
  • Acrobatic – High resistance to knockdowns; performs acrobatic maneuvers on good rolls
  • Frenzy – Can unleash a burst of extra attacks once per fight
  • Spear Exception – Spears are exempt from their heavy weapon penalty
  • Light Frame – Suffers penalty with heavy weapons (except spears)
  • Frail – Very low maximum HP and Strength

Favorite Weapons: Dagger, Short Sword, Scimitar, Epee, Stiletto, Hatchet, Javelin, Scythe, Short Spear

Weak Weapons: Great Axe, Great Sword, Halberd, Maul, Battle Flail (too heavy; suffer penalties)

Special Rules: Spears (Short Spear, Boar Spear, Long Spear, Javelin) are exempt from the heavy weapon penalty, allowing Tabaxi to use them without penalty.

Typical Build: Maximize Dexterity, moderate Strength. Dodge/Evasion specialist. Light weapons only, or spears. The Frenzy ability grants a burst of speed mid-fight (triggered automatically) to finish a weakened opponent, or to turn the tides of a fight. Goal: Dance around the opponent, dodge everything, land many small hits.

Fighting Styles Matrix

BLOODSPIRE applies 15 specific fighting configurations altering underlying simulation math and active combat behavior.

Select a tactical style configuration from the dropdown menu to examine mechanical values.

Total Kill (Aggressive)

  • Goal: Destroy the opponent; maximum offense, no defense
  • Special: Ignores all defensive effort; defense rolls are significantly reduced
  • Use: Only when you have a clear health advantage and want to end the fight fast
  • Weapons: Prefer heavy weapons (Great Axe, War Flail, Maul, Great Pick)
  • Risk: If you're losing, this guarantees faster defeat

Wall of Steel (Defensive/Counter)

  • Goal: Form an impenetrable wall; block everything
  • Mechanics: High parry bonus, increases parry attempts
  • Use: When facing aggressive opponents; accumulate damage-free turns
  • Weapons: Shields (especially Tower Shield, Target Shield), Short Sword, Boar Spear
  • Risk: Low offense; opponent may outlast you

Lunge (Precision)

  • Goal: Thrust with precision; bonus accuracy and reach
  • Special: Accuracy bonus per Lunge skill level; Long Spear, Boar Spear, Javelin favor this
  • Use: Finesse-focused warriors; effective at range
  • Weapons: Spears (Short/Boar/Long), Epee, Stiletto, Scimitar
  • Affinity: Powerful when combined with high Dexterity

Bash (Power)

  • Goal: Blunt force trauma; heavy impact
  • Special: Bonus with blunt weapons (Hammer, Mace, Morningstar, Club)
  • Use: When facing light armor; can knock down opponents
  • Weapons: Blunt weapons (Hammer, Morningstar, War Hammer, Maul)
  • Affinity: Half-Orcs with War Hammer or Maul

Slash (Aggression)

  • Goal: Sweeping cuts; moderate damage
  • Use: Sword and axe specialists; balanced offense/defense
  • Weapons: Broad Sword, Bastard Sword, Great Sword, Battle Axe, Great Axe, Scythe
  • Affinity: Half-Elves with Bastard Sword; Elves with Scythe

Strike (Core)

  • Goal: Straightforward stabbing and cutting; balanced
  • Use: Foundation style for many builds
  • Weapons: Versatile (Short Sword, Longsword, Mace, Hammer, Boar Spear)
  • Affinity: Works with almost any warrior/weapon combo

Engage & Withdraw (Evasion)

  • Goal: Attack then retreat; hit-and-run tactics
  • Special: Bonus damage/accuracy when performing tactical retreats
  • Use: Against heavy opponents you're outmatched against
  • Weapons: Light weapons (Dagger, Scimitar, Javelin)
  • Affinity: Tabaxi, Elves, Halflings

Counterstrike (Reaction)

  • Goal: Let opponent attack, then retaliate
  • Special: Bonus damage/accuracy when responding to attacks
  • Use: Defensive warriors (Dwarf + Target Shield + Long Sword)
  • Weapons: Medium weapons paired with shields (Longsword + Target Shield)
  • Affinity: Gnomes with Counterstrike Mastery ability

Decoy (Misdirection)

  • Goal: Feint and deceive; confuse opponent
  • Special: Feint attack (moderate chance to land); canceled by Counterstrike opponents
  • Use: Against opponents who predict your moves
  • Weapons: Medium weapons (Short Sword, Longsword, Epee)
  • Risk: Feint fails against clever opponents

Sure Strike (Accuracy)

  • Goal: Guaranteed accuracy; no misses (very high defense bypass)
  • Special: Always hits; defense rolls are cancelled
  • Use: When accuracy matters more than damage
  • Weapons: Missile weapons (Net, Bola), finesse weapons
  • Affinity: High Intelligence for precision calculation

Calculated Attack (Precision)

  • Goal: Surgical precision; threads a seam in the armor
  • Special: Bonus armor bypass based on Dexterity and Intelligence
  • Use: Finesse builds facing heavily-armored opponents
  • Weapons: Stiletto, Dagger, Epee, Small Pick, Military Pick
  • Affinity: High Dexterity + Intelligence

Martial Combat (Unarmed)

  • Goal: Fight with open hand or martial weapons
  • Special: Bonus damage with unarmed, Open Hand, Dagger, etc.; scales with Martial combat skill
  • MC-Compatible Weapons: Stiletto, Dagger, Knife, Quarterstaff, Net, Great Staff, Open Hand
  • Use: Halflings, Lizardfolk, Half-Elves with high Martial Combat skill
  • Affinity: Races with Martial Combat Bonus; Open Hand scales infinitely with Strength

Opportunity Throw (Ranged)

  • Goal: Throw weapons at distance
  • Special: Uses Throw skill; reduces Strength contribution to damage
  • Special Goblins: Bonus to Opportunity Throw; throwables weigh less for them
  • Throwable Weapons: Stiletto, Knife, Dagger, Hatchet, Francisca, Short Spear, Boar Spear, Javelin, Hammer and Bola
  • Use: Open-range fighting; avoid close-in brawls
  • Risk: Throwable weapons run out if not managed

Parry (Defense)

  • Goal: Block everything; active defense
  • Special: High parry rolls; bonus to blocking weapons
  • Use: Defensive warriors who want to reduce damage taken
  • Weapons: Medium weapons, especially shields
  • Affinity: Dwarves, high Parry skill

Defend (Protection)

  • Goal: Minimize injury; damage reduction
  • Special: Reduces incoming damage by a flat amount (armor improved)
  • Use: Warriors who are taking consistent damage
  • Weapons: Shields (especially tower shields)
  • Affinity: Dwarves with Full Plate + Tower Shield

Special Armor Rules

Armor Penalty: If your Strength is insufficient for the armor weight, you suffer a proportional attack rate and accuracy penalty.

Dex Penalty: Armor reduces your effective Dexterity for dodge and initiative. This can be mitigated by training Dexterity to counter the penalty.

Armor-Piercing (AP) Vulnerability: Some weapons (Stiletto, Picks, Scythe, Great Pick) do bonus damage vs Scale, Chain, Half-Plate, and Full Plate. Against AP-vulnerable armor, these weapons' armor reduction is halved (defense is cut in half when calculating damage reduction).

Dwarf Rule: Dwarves can wear one tier of body armor heavier than their Strength normally allows without penalty.

Lizardfolk Rule: Natural scales provide inherent defense. Light armor (Cloth, Leather) can add a small bonus, but heavier armor provides no additional protection while still imposing movement penalties. Recommended: No armor, Cloth, or Leather only.

Trigger Ordering & Strategy System

What is a Trigger?

A trigger is a condition that activates a strategy row. Each minute, the game evaluates your strategy rows top-to-bottom and selects the FIRST one whose trigger condition is true. That strategy (style, activity level, aiming, defense) is then used for the entire minute.

Critical: Order matters. Triggers are read from top to bottom; place your most specific triggers first and your fallback (Always) last.

All Triggers (32+ total, in evaluation order)

Listed in the order they are checked:

  1. None – This trigger never activates; skip the row
  2. Minute 1 through Minute 10 – Activates only in that specific minute (Minute 1-10)
  3. You are very tired – Your endurance is very low
  4. Your foe is very tired – Opponent endurance is very low
  5. You are somewhat tired – Your endurance is moderately low
  6. Your foe is somewhat tired – Opponent endurance is moderately low
  7. You are slightly tired – Your endurance is a bit low
  8. Your foe is slightly tired – Opponent endurance is a bit low
  9. You have taken heavy damage – Your HP is critically low
  10. Your foe has taken heavy damage – Opponent HP is critically low
  11. You have taken medium damage – Your HP is moderately low
  12. Your foe has taken medium damage – Opponent HP is moderately low
  13. You have taken slight damage – Your HP is a bit low
  14. Your foe has taken slight damage – Opponent HP is a bit low
  15. You challenged your foe – You initiated the fight (if applicable)
  16. Your foe challenged you – Opponent initiated
  17. You blood challenged your foe – You initiated with high Presence hesitation roll success
  18. Your foe blood challenged you – Opponent initiated with high Presence
  19. You are on the ground – You've been knocked down
  20. Your foe is on the ground – Opponent knocked down
  21. You are weaponless – Primary weapon has been disarmed/dropped
  22. Your foe is weaponless – Opponent has no weapon
  23. You have no throwable weapons – All Opportunity Throw weapons have been used
  24. You have at least one throwable weapon – At least one throwable in inventory
  25. You have exactly one throwable weapon – Exactly one left
  26. You have exactly one weapon – Only primary, secondary is Open Hand
  27. You have at least 2 weapons – Both primary and secondary are weapons (no shields)
  28. You have more than 2 weapons – Carrying backup weapon in addition to equipped pair
  29. Your foe is wearing light armor – Cloth or Leather
  30. Your foe is wearing medium armor – Cuir Boulli, Brigandine, or Scale
  31. Your foe is wearing heavy armor – Chain, Half-Plate, or Full Plate
  32. Always (Default Loop) – Fallback; always true if nothing above matches

Strategy Components (in each row)

Each strategy row consists of:

Component Options Purpose
Trigger 32 conditions (above) When to use this row

Activity 0–9 Aggression level (0=defensive, 5=balanced, 9=all-out)

Optimal Trigger Ordering

Strategy Principle: Place specific, high-priority triggers first; fallback triggers last.

Example Strategy Table (generic warrior):

# Trigger Style Activity Aim Defense
1 You are on the ground Wall of Steel 3 Chest Head
2 You have no throwable weapons Wall of Steel 5 None Chest
3 You are very tired Defend 2 None Chest
4 Your foe is very tired Total Kill 8 Head Chest
5 You have taken heavy damage Parry 4 None Chest
6 Always Strike 5 Chest Chest

Rationale:

  • Row 1 (Ground): If knocked down, protect yourself (Wall of Steel, low activity)
  • Row 2 (Out of throwables): If you can't throw anymore, switch to a safer style
  • Row 3 (Very tired): Save endurance; go defensive
  • Row 4 (Foe very tired): Enemy is weak; press the advantage (Total Kill)
  • Row 5 (Heavy damage): You're hurt; focus on survival
  • Row 6 (Always): Default; use this if nothing else triggered

Special Trigger Mechanics

  • Activity Level (0–9):
    • 0–2: Very passive; dodge/defend; opponents get initiative
    • 3–4: Defensive; good for cautious builds
    • 5: Balanced; typical middle-ground
    • 6–7: Aggressive; chase opponents
    • 8–9: All-out; ignore defense, attack only
  • Aim Point (targeting injuries):
    • None: Hit whatever's vulnerable (random)
    • Head, Chest, Abdomen, Arm, Leg: Target that specific location for injury
    • Aimed attacks may fail if the opponent dodges/parries, but successful hits target that location
  • Defense Point (protection):
    • When your defense roll beats the attack roll by the margin listed as "defense" in your strategy, your defense point gets a significant bonus
    • Example: If your strategy lists "Defense: Chest" and you parry successfully, Chest gets a bonus to the parry roll
    • Decoy Style note: When opponent uses Decoy, your defense point bonus is canceled (the feint nullifies it). Use Counterstrike to read and negate Decoy's misdirection.

Trigger Advantages & Disadvantages

  • Minute Triggers (Minute 1–10):
    • Advantage: Very predictable; plan exactly what you'll do each minute
    • Disadvantage: You don't adapt if the fight changes unexpectedly
  • Health/Endurance Triggers:
    • Advantage: Adapt dynamically as the fight progresses
    • Disadvantage: If the opponent also uses these, they counter your strategy
  • Weapon Triggers:
    • Advantage: Manage weapon depletion (Opportunity Throw users)
    • Disadvantage: Requires careful weapon management pre-fight
  • Always (Fallback):
    • Advantage: Safe default; always used if nothing else matches
    • Disadvantage: Unresponsive to changing circumstances

Advanced Combat Mechanics

Hit Resolution

Each attack is resolved as:

  1. Attacker rolls: d100 + DEX_bonus + weapon_skill*5 + luck + style_mod + feint_bonus
  2. Defender rolls: d100 + STR_or_DEX_bonus + parry/dodge_skill4 + weapon_skillbonus + luck
  3. Margin = Attack - Defense. Margin ≤ 0: Miss/Parry/Dodge (no damage). Margin 1–9: Graze (minor damage, no effects). Margin ≥ 10: Hit (full damage calculation).

Damage Calculation

Damage is based on:

  • Weapon damage range (e.g., Boar Spear: 15–18)
  • STR bonus (warrior.strength - 10)
  • Weapon skill level (0–9, adds flat bonus)
  • Margin scaling (higher margin = higher damage fraction of weapon's max)
  • Armor reduction (percentage based on defense points)
  • Special bonuses (racial bonuses, style bonuses, favorite weapon bonus)

Concede System

At low HP, the opponent may concede (surrender). The Pitmaster grants mercy based on:

  • Concede roll = d100 + PRE_bonus + luck modifiers
  • Threshold: Opponent's presence determines how easily they concede
  • Monster fights: Monsters never concede; always fight to the death

Death Check

If a warrior drops to significantly negative HP (overkill), there's a chance they die:

  • Death chance increases the more negative the HP
  • If death triggers, the warrior is slain; if not, they take a permanent injury instead

Luck System

Every warrior has a Luck factor (1–30) assigned at creation. This is added to EVERY roll (attack, defense, initiative, skill training, concede). Lucky warriors consistently punch above their weight; unlucky ones may underperform despite good stats.
