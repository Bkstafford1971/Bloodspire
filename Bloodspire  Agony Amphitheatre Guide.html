Part I

Getting Started

Welcome to Bloodspire

Bloodspire is not a simple game. It is a living arena of warriors, each with their own stats, histories, wounds, and dreams of glory. You are a manager — a director of champions, a builder of legends, a strategist on the grandest stage. Your warriors will bleed, will triumph, will sometimes fall. And you will decide their fate.

The Agony Amphitheatre is the heart of the arena — the normal arena where warriors begin their careers and where the bulk of the action happens. It is brutal, competitive, and unforgiving. It is also where the greatest stories are born.

This guide covers everything you need to know to compete effectively in the normal arena.

Commitment matters. Every choice echoes through your warrior's career.

The Three-Arena Hierarchy

Bloodspire features three distinct arenas:

  • The Agony Amphitheatre (Normal Arena): Where your career begins. Standard warriors compete for recognition and glory.
  • The Elite Spire: The pinnacle for living warriors. Only the most accomplished are called here.
  • The Soul Spire: Where the mightiest fallen are reborn. Only warriors who die with legendary records transcend here.

This guide focuses on the Normal Arena. If your warriors ever ascend to the advanced arenas, a separate guide covers those systems.

Your Role as Manager

As a manager, you control:

  • Team composition: Which 5 warriors you field each turn
  • Equipment: What weapons, armor, and helms your warriors wield
  • Strategy: How your warriors fight (tactics, styles, triggers)
  • Training: What skills and attributes your warriors develop
  • Challenges: Which opponents you seek out
  • Special requests: Retirement, monster hunts, and personal avoidances

You do NOT control:

  • Fight outcomes (those are determined by combat engine)
  • Opponent selection (matchmaking uses your tier and history)
  • Individual combat decisions (strategies fire automatically)

  • Scouting Tab: Scout specific warriors to gather intelligence reports
  • Legacy Tab: Archived teams and historical records (replaced teams only)

  • Shady Pines Tab: Retirement home for retired warriors (if applicable)

The Upload Process

Bloodspire operates on a turn cycle:

  • OPEN: You can make changes and upload your teams. This is your window to adjust before fights happen.
  • PROCESSING: The arena is running. Fights are taking place. No uploads allowed.
  • RESULTS READY: All fights are done. Results available. Next turn's OPEN window begins.

Multiple Teams

Managers can control up to 5 teams simultaneously, with 5 warriors per team (up to 25 warriors total). Each team uploads independently.

What You Upload

When you upload a team, you send the server:

  • Your 5 active warriors' complete state per team
  • All gear, strategies, training queues per warrior
  • Personal preferences (avoidances, retirements, monster requests, Run This Turn flags)

Validation & Re-uploading

The server validates all uploads to prevent cheating (checks stats, equipment legality, training consistency, etc.). Your team is locked in when the turn RUNS, but you can make modifications and re-upload as many times as needed during the OPEN phase. This allows you to:

  • Change strategies after researching opponents
  • Adjust training queues based on recent results
  • Modify equipment loadouts
  • Update challenge requests
  • Re-submit if you made a mistake

Keep uploading and adjusting until you're satisfied. Only when the turn begins (PROCESSING phase) is everything locked in.

Part II

Character Creation

Building Your First Warrior

Every warrior begins the same way: you design them from scratch. Name them. Choose their race. Distribute your starting attributes. Equip them with basic gear. And send them into the arena.

This is the most important decision you'll make. Your choices here shape your warrior's entire career path.

The Six Core Attributes

Every warrior is defined by six base attributes. The rollup process starts with 55 base points distributed across all 6 attributes (range 3-25 per stat), then you add 16 points to customize (max +7 per stat). Your final attribute total is 71 points. Warriors can improve attributes later through training (except SIZ, which is fixed forever).

Strength STR

Your warrior's raw physical power.

  • High STR = more damage, ability to carry heavy weapons and armor
  • Low STR = lighter hits, restrictions on equipment weight
  • Warriors with insufficient STR to wield equipment suffer penalties
Dexterity DEX

Speed, coordination, and reflexes.

  • High DEX = bonus to initiative rolls, better accuracy, higher dodge chance
  • Low DEX = penalty to initiative rolls, worse accuracy, easier to hit
  • The most combat-relevant stat for action frequency in combat
Constitution CON

Endurance and physical resilience.

  • High CON = more HP, more stamina, better resistance to injury
  • Low CON = fragile, tires quickly, injury-prone
  • Affects training success for attributes
Intelligence INT

Learning speed and mental acuity.

  • High INT = skills train faster and more reliably, better tactical grasp
  • Low INT = slower learning, higher chance of training failure
  • Affects training success for all skills
Presence PRE

Charisma and force of personality.

  • High PRE = better chance of intimidating foes at fight start, higher recognition gains
  • Low PRE = harder to cow opponents, slower fame accumulation
  • Determines concede appeal chances (mercy when badly wounded)
Size SIZ

Physical dimensions and frame.

  • High SIZ = more HP, better weight capacity for equipment
  • Low SIZ = fragile, can't carry as much gear
  • CANNOT be trained later — fixed at creation forever

Distributing Your Points

Character creation happens in two stages:

  • Stage 1 - Random Rollup: Your warrior gets a random distribution of 55 points across all 6 attributes. Each attribute starts between 3 and 25. You cannot control this rollup — it's determined by chance. (This is where the re-roll option from the Replacement Tab comes in handy if you don't like the numbers.)
  • Stage 2 - Customization: You receive 16 additional points to allocate strategically. You can add up to 7 points to any single attribute to shape your build. Your final total across all 6 attributes will be 71 points.

Build Strategy: Your Combat Identity

Your stat distribution choices define how your warrior fights. Think strategically about what role you want them to fill:

Aggressive · Damage Dealer

High STR + High DEX

  • Strength: Hit hard and hit often. Win fights quickly before endurance becomes a factor.
  • Weakness: Fragile. Low CON means fewer HP and easier to injure. Struggles in long grinds.
  • Strategy fit: Fast-paced aggressive styles (Total Kill, Lunge, Bash). Light armor for mobility.
  • Playstyle: Dominate early minutes. Risk everything on decisive striking.
  • Risk: If opponent survives the onslaught and tires you out, you lose.
Defensive · Tank

High CON + High SIZ

  • Strength: Absorb punishment. Outlast opponents in attrition fights. Hard to kill.
  • Weakness: Slower actions per minute. Fewer chances to attack. May get outlasted by faster opponents.
  • Strategy fit: Defensive styles (Parry, Defend, Counterstrike). Heavy armor for protection.
  • Playstyle: Survive and respond. Let opponent exhaust themselves. Win via superior endurance.
  • Risk: Cannot close out fights quickly. If opponent is also tanky, fights become stalemates.
Tactical · Genius

High INT + Balanced others

  • Strength: Train faster. Learn new skills quicker than rivals. Outsmart through preparation.
  • Weakness: No immediate combat advantage. Early career is slower.
  • Strategy fit: Complex strategy designs with multiple triggers. Adaptive responses.
  • Playstyle: Invest in knowledge. Build elaborate strategy trees. Control fights through adaptation.
  • Risk: Raw stats matter in arena. Smarts don't substitute for physical capability.
Charismatic · Leader

High PRE + Moderate others

  • Strength: Gain recognition faster. Intimidate opponents at fight start. Better concede appeal chances.
  • Weakness: PRE doesn't directly win fights. Social advantages are secondary.
  • Strategy fit: Works with any style, but shines with champion-hunt strategies.
  • Playstyle: Climb reputation quickly. Challenge upward. Become famous.
  • Risk: Fame without capability gets you knocked down. PRE helps but doesn't guarantee wins.
Versatile · Balanced

Moderate everything

  • Strength: Flexible. Can adapt to any playstyle. No glaring weaknesses.
  • Weakness: Master of none. No standout advantage. Slower specialization than focused builds.
  • Strategy fit: Works with standard strategies. Versatile gear choices.
  • Playstyle: Jack-of-all-trades approach. Steady, reliable progression.
  • Risk: Specialized opponents will outperform you in their domain.

Making Your Choice

Ask yourself:

  • Do I want to win fights fast or win through endurance?
  • Do I prefer reactive defense or proactive offense?
  • Do I want to climb tiers quickly or build a legendary warrior?
  • What strategy am I comfortable designing — simple or complex?

Your stat distribution shapes your answer. There is no "correct" build — only builds that match your playstyle and strategy. Test different combinations across multiple warriors. Over time you'll discover what resonates with you.

Race Selection

Ten playable races exist, each with a distinct identity and combat flavor.

Each race brings inherent bonuses and penalties to combat. These are NOT numerical bonuses you can see — they are woven into how damage is calculated, how armor works, how their weapons land. You will discover them through play.

Human

The baseline. Versatile. Middle-ground stats and capabilities. No particular strength or weakness.

Half-Orc

Physically powerful but socially awkward. Stronger damage output, but dodges less reliably.

Halfling

Small and nimble. Excellent dodge and evasion. Weaker physical hits.

Dwarf

Tough and stubborn. Exceptional HP and armor resilience. Can wear heavier gear than other races. Slower.

Half-Elf

Versatile and capable. Good all-around stats with slight edge in weapon handling. No major weaknesses or strengths.

Elf

Elegant and deadly. Masters of dual-wielding. Exceptional dodge and evasion. Extremely fragile with weaker raw damage.

Goblin

Cunning and fast. High initiative and action frequency. Fragile.

Gnome

Clever and quick. Similar to Goblins but slightly more well-rounded. Trains quickly.

Lizardfolk

Exotic and alien. Different combat feel. Thick scales provide armor-like resilience.

Tabaxi

Feline warrior. Excellent reflexes and speed. Unique flavor in combat.

Each race has a visual identity. Your warrior's appearance reflects their heritage. This is narrative flavor — part of the fun is seeing how your chosen race looks in action.

Gender & Appearance

Choose male or female. This is cosmetic only — no combat mechanical difference. It affects your warrior's appearance, height, and weight (within the natural variance for your race and size).

The Luck Factor (A Mystery)

Every warrior is born with an invisible luck value between 1 and 30. You cannot see this. You cannot influence it at creation. It is pure chance.

Luck affects how your warrior performs relative to their stats. Lucky warriors (25-30) seem to perform better than numbers suggest. Unlucky warriors (1-5) underperform. Most warriors (10-20) perform exactly as expected.

Over time, your warriors' true luck reveals itself in their fight records. Some warriors seem blessed. Others cursed. This is Bloodspire's randomness — the element that keeps the game fresh and unpredictable.

Naming Your Warrior

Choose a name. Be creative, be serious, be silly — your warrior carries this name forever (unless they die and are replaced).

Naming Rules:

  • Names must be 25 characters or fewer
  • Avoid numbers and special characters
  • Keep it pronounceable
  • This name appears in newsletters and fight results

Your First Warrior is Ready

Once created, your warrior is locked in. They will be added to your roster immediately. Next turn, when fights happen, they will fight.

You cannot edit a warrior once created. You cannot reset their attributes or change their race. The only way to start over is to let them die and create a replacement.

This is by design. Commitment matters.

Team Management

Your Roster

You manage exactly 5 warriors per team at all times. No more, no fewer per team.

Since you can manage up to 5 teams simultaneously, you could have up to 25 active warriors total (5 teams × 5 warriors). But each individual team always maintains exactly 5 warriors.

When a warrior dies, retires, ascends to the Monster roster, or is promoted to the Elite Spire arena, their slot opens on that team. You must create a replacement warrior to fill the empty slot.

Important: If you do not upload a replacement, your team cannot fight. Empty slots prevent that team from participating in turns until filled with a new warrior.

The Upload Cycle

After you make any changes (new warrior, gear update, strategy change, training queue), you must upload your team to the server in order for those changes to be applied.

Important: Changes made locally in your client do not take effect until you upload them to the server. The server only knows about the last version you uploaded.

When you upload:

  • You send the server your entire team's state
  • The server validates it (checks for cheating, wrong stats, tampered records)
  • This upload becomes the version the server will use for that team when the turn runs
  • You can upload as many times as you want during the OPEN phase — each new upload overwrites the previous one

What happens if you don't upload:

  • Your last upload's state is used for that team (provided you have 5 active, living warriors)
  • If a team has an empty slot (dead/retired/promoted warrior), that team cannot fight until you create a replacement and upload
  • Per-team uploads: Each team uploads independently. You could have 4 complete teams fighting and 1 team sitting out waiting for a replacement — the 4 complete teams still fight even if the 5th team is incomplete

After the turn:

  • Download your results
  • You'll see the updated state of all your warriors (new wins/losses, recognition changes, training success/failure, new injuries)
  • Any deaths are recorded
  • Your local copy is updated with server's version

Uploads are the heartbeat of Bloodspire. They lock in your decisions. Treat them seriously.

Equipping Your Warriors

Every warrior has four gear slots:

  • Primary Weapon: Your main fighting tool. Can be any of 44 weapons, or bare fists (Open Hand). Affects damage output and attack speed.
  • Secondary Weapon: Your off-hand tool. Can be a shield, another weapon, or bare fists.
  • Helm: Head protection. Ranges from none to full helm. Provides defense to head location, but can restrict vision.
  • Armor: Body protection. Ranges from cloth to full plate. Provides broad defense but adds weight and sluggishness.

Weapons: The 48 Tools of War

You have 48 weapons to choose from, organized by category:

  • Swords & Knives: Fast, accurate, classic. Good all-around damage and action frequency.
  • Axes & Picks: Medium speed, good damage, armor-piercing (ignores heavy armor defenses).
  • Hammers & Maces: Slower, crushing damage, good for knockdowns and stunning.
  • Polearms & Spears: Long reach, defensive, some can perform charge attacks.
  • Flails: Medium speed, bypass shields (wrap around defenses), can sweep.
  • Staves: Slow, high damage, good for sweeping attacks, good for defensive styles.
  • Shields: Can be primary or off-hand. Increase parry chance and defense rolls. Deal damage on hit and enable shield rushes and shield bashes. Versatile defensive tool.
  • Oddities: Nets, cestus, and rare weapons with unique properties.

Each weapon has weight, speed, damage, and special properties. Your warrior's STR determines if they can wield each weapon without penalty.

Strength Requirements: Higher STR = access to heavier weapons. A warrior with STR 8 cannot effectively wield a full plate or two-handed axe (too heavy). They suffer accuracy and speed penalties. Your gear should match your stats.

Armor & Helms: Defense vs. Mobility Trade-off

Armor provides defense (damage reduction) but adds weight and sluggishness.

Armor Progression

  • Cloth/Leather: Light, fast, minimal defense
  • Cuir Boulli/Brigandine: Medium, moderate defense
  • Scale/Chain: Heavy, good defense, moves slower
  • Half-Plate/Full Plate: Maximum defense, maximum weight penalty

Helm Progression

  • None/Cap: No defense, maximum mobility
  • Steel Cap/Helm: Light defense
  • Camail/Full Helm: Maximum defense, vision restricted

Heavier armor adds penalties to initiative rolls, causing slower reactions. Light armor allows faster initiative rolls. Match armor to your strategy: aggressive warriors benefit from light gear for better initiative, defensive warriors accept slower reactions in exchange for defense.

Match armor to your strategy. Aggressive warriors want light gear. Defensive warriors want heavy.

Dwarf Exception: Dwarves can equip one tier of armor above their normal STR limit without penalty. This is their racial advantage — they're built for heavy metal.

Backup Weapons

Optionally, carry a third weapon as backup. If your primary or secondary weapon breaks or is disarmed during a fight, you automatically draw your backup.

This is situational. Most warriors skip it. Some defensive builds include one.

Challenge Strategies

Challenge Mode is optional. It allows you to build an alternate strategy table that activates only if your challenge is successful.

How it works:

  • You enable the Challenge Mode checkbox for a warrior
  • You fill in a separate strategy table (challenge strategies)
  • If that warrior successfully gets a challenge match (the challenge is accepted), those challenge strategies are used for that fight
  • If the challenge is not accepted or the warrior faces other opponents, the normal strategy table is used instead

Example: Warrior Bob decides to challenge Warrior Joe. Bob wants to use an aggressive strategy specifically tuned for Joe for that challenge match. Bob enables Challenge Mode and builds a special strategy for that matchup. If Bob gets the challenge match against Joe, the challenge strategies fire for that fight. If the challenge isn't accepted or Bob faces a different opponent, Bob's normal strategies govern the fight.

This is useful for preparing special tactics against known rivals.

Manager Avoidance: Dodging Rivals

You can avoid up to 2 opposing managers. The system tries to prevent auto-matchups against them and also works to prevent those managers from successfully challenging you (roughly 25-30% success rate).

This does not guarantee you'll never face them. Matchmaking is complex. But it reduces the likelihood of matchups against avoided managers through both random pairing and challenge prevention.

Important: Avoidances remain in your list until you manually remove them. They do not reset between turns. Update your avoidance list whenever you want to change who you're avoiding.

Warrior Avoidance: Dodging Specific Foes

Each warrior can avoid up to 2 opposing warriors by name. This prevents them from being directly challenged by those foes, but does not prevent random matchmaking.

Use this to avoid opponents who have a specific counter to your build.

Part IV

Understanding Combat

The Arena and the Spectacle

When your warrior steps into the Agony Amphitheatre, they face one of six possible opponents:

  1. Standard Match: Random peer warrior
  2. Challenge: Opponent you or they chose directly
  3. Blood Challenge: Vengeance fight available after a teammate is killed (within 3-turn window)
  4. Champion Challenge: High-ranking warrior (EXPERT or ELITE tier) challenging the sitting champion
  5. Monster Fight: Fight against a legendary creature. Risky, high-reward scenario used to test a warrior's true strength or eliminate underperforming warriors. Victory means ascension to the Monster roster; defeat means death.
  6. Peasant Bout: Filler match when no real opponents available

Tier Progression (approx.):

  • RECRUIT: Your newest warriors, 0-12 recognition
  • ROOKIE: Early-career warriors, 13-25 recognition
  • INITIATE: Developing warriors, 26-40 recognition
  • ADEPT: Competent warriors, 41-55 recognition
  • VETERAN: Seasoned warriors, 56-70 recognition
  • EXPERT: Accomplished warriors, 71-86 recognition
  • ELITE: Elite warriors, 87-99 recognition
  • CHAMPION: Determined by an external flag (is_champion), not tied to recognition score. Can be achieved at any recognition level by defeating the sitting champion.

Matching Rules:

  • You can challenge your own tier or one tier above
  • Matchmaking tries to pair similar experience levels
  • Avoids rematches from the last 3 turns when possible
  • Champion challenges are restricted to the top 2 populated tiers in the arena (Blood Challenges can bypass this restriction if a deceased teammate's killer is the champion)

Climbing tiers takes time. Your first warriors will spend their early career in RECRUIT, earning respect fight by fight.

The Fight Itself

Most fights are completed in under 5 minutes. After 7 minutes, the referee will begin to intervene to try to speed up the process.

Combat flow:

  1. Warrior strategies are evaluated for triggering conditions
  2. Initiative rolls determine who attacks on each action
  3. Attacking warrior attempts to hit defending warrior
  4. Defending warrior attempts to dodge or parry
  5. If hit lands, damage is calculated and applied
  6. Warriors take actions based on their active strategy
  7. Endurance (stamina) is consumed with each action
  8. Process continues until a warrior falls to exhaustion, dies, or successfully concedes

Speed & Action Frequency:
Warriors with high DEX and trained initiative skills have better bonuses on initiative rolls. Initiative rolls determine who attacks on each action, so higher DEX increases your chances to attack first and control the pace of combat.

Combat Mechanics: The Hidden Dance

Hit & Miss

Your attack succeeds or fails based on the margin between your attack roll and their defense roll. These rolls incorporate:

  • Weapon skill levels
  • Base attributes (DEX for accuracy, STR for weapon control)
  • Armor penalties (heavy armor makes dodging harder)
  • Strategy choices (defensive strategies are harder to hit)

Damage

When you hit, damage is calculated based on:

  • Your weapon (base damage varies widely)
  • Your STR (higher STR = more damage)
  • Weapon skill level (trained skills hit harder)
  • Opponent's armor (heavy armor blocks more damage)

Knockdowns

Certain weapons and fighting styles can knock your opponent down. The knockdown action itself causes damage (minimal but meaningful). Being on the ground doesn't cause continuous damage, but multiple knockdowns throughout a fight can add up. Ground-fighting is brief but can turn momentum. Knockdowns matter more in longer fights where repeated knockdowns accumulate damage.

Injuries

When you get hit hard enough, you can suffer injuries to specific body locations (head, chest, arms, legs, etc.). Injuries persist and reduce combat effectiveness. Worse injuries (level 7-8) are severely crippling. Level 9 injuries are fatal (warrior dies immediately).

Endurance (Stamina)

Each action burns endurance. Higher activity levels burn faster. As endurance depletes, your actions slow and accuracy suffers. You become vulnerable.

Weapon Disarm

Certain weapons (nets, swordbreaker, ball-and-chain) can disarm opponents. A disarmed warrior loses weapon damage but can still fight with fists.

Armor-Piercing

Certain weapons (picks, scythes) ignore heavy armor defenses. If your opponent is in full plate and you wield an armor-piercing weapon, the armor is less protective.

Strategy: The Brain of Your Warrior

Your warriors follow pre-programmed strategies that fire automatically based on fight conditions.

Strategy Structure:
Each strategy has 5 components:

  1. Trigger: WHEN does this strategy activate?
  2. Fighting Style: HOW does your warrior fight when this triggers?
  3. Activity Level: AGGRESSION level (0-9)
  4. Aim Point: WHERE do they try to hit?
  5. Defense Point: WHERE do they prioritize protecting?

Triggers (41 total)

  • Time-based: "During minute 1-10" (specific minute triggers)

Fatigue-based:

  • "You are very tired" (75% endurance burned)
  • "You are somewhat tired" (50% endurance burned)
  • "You are slightly tired" (25% endurance burned)
  • "Opponent is very tired" (opponent's 75% burned)
  • Similar for "somewhat" and "slightly"

Damage-based:

  • "You have taken heavy damage" (50%+ HP lost)
  • "You have taken medium damage" (20-50% HP lost)
  • "You have taken slight damage" (5-20% HP lost)
  • "Your opponent has taken heavy damage" (50%+ HP lost)
  • "Your opponent has taken medium damage" (20-50% HP lost)
  • "Your opponent has taken slight damage" (5-20% HP lost)

Combat State:

  • "You are on the ground" (after knockdown)
  • "You are weaponless" (weapon disarmed or dropped)
  • "You have specific weapon equipped" (sword, axe, etc.)
  • "You have throwable weapons" (0, 1, or 2+)

Opponent Armor:

  • "Your opponent is wearing light armor"
  • "Your opponent is wearing medium armor"
  • "Your opponent is wearing heavy armor"

Fight Type:

  • "You have been challenged" (responding to specific challenge)
  • "You initiated a challenge"
  • "Opponent blood-challenged you"

Always (Fallback): Matches if nothing above triggers

Fighting Styles (15 total)

Aggressive Styles (8):

Total Kill Wall of Steel Lunge Bash Slash Strike Martial Combat Opportunity Throw
  • Total Kill: Pure offense, all-in damage
  • Wall of Steel: Constant attacks, shield bashing
  • Lunge: High-risk charging attacks
  • Bash: Hammer/mace focused, knockdown attempts
  • Slash: Blade-focused, deep cuts
  • Strike: Precise strong hits
  • Martial Combat: Unarmed/brawling focus
  • Opportunity Throw: Ranged thrown-weapon focus

Defensive Styles (7):

Parry Defend Sure Strike Calculated Attack Counterstrike Engage & Withdraw Decoy
  • Parry: Active blade-defense
  • Defend: Cautious, damage-avoidance focused
  • Sure Strike: Conservative accurate hits
  • Calculated Attack: Methodical, less frequent attacks
  • Counterstrike: Wait-and-react, punish openings
  • Engage & Withdraw: Hit-and-run tactics
  • Decoy: Confuse and evade

Activity Level (0-9)

  • 0 = Minimal actions, extreme defense, burns little endurance
  • 5 = Balanced (default)
  • 9 = Maximum actions, extreme offense, burns endurance fast

Activity level influences your initiative rolls each minute. Higher activity levels improve your initiative roll (faster reactions, more attacks). Lower activity levels worsen your initiative roll (slower reactions, fewer attacks) but conserve endurance.

Aim & Defense Points

Head, Chest, Abdomen, Primary Arm, Secondary Arm, Primary Leg, Secondary Leg, None (no specific focus)

Choose where you try to strike (aim) and where you prioritize protection (defense). Some strategies focus on specific locations.

How Strategies Work in Practice

Strategies are evaluated top-to-bottom. The first matching trigger activates that strategy, and it stays active until a higher-order strategy's trigger becomes true and overrides it.

Important: Trigger order matters. If you have overlapping triggers (like "Heavy Damage" and "Medium Damage"), you must order them carefully:

  • Triggers further UP the list have priority over triggers below them
  • A trigger lower in the list won't fire if a trigger above it would catch the same condition
  • Example: If you list "Medium Damage" (50%+ HP lost) ABOVE "Heavy Damage" (75%+ HP lost), the medium damage trigger will catch both cases. Heavy damage will never fire. Reverse the order so Heavy fires first when met.

Example Scenario

Warrior has 3 strategies (in order):

  • Strat 1: "When opponent heavily armored" → Bash, Activity 7, Aim: Legs
  • Strat 2: "When you've taken 50%+ damage" → Defend, Activity 2, Aim: None
  • Strat 3: "Always" → Strike, Activity 5, Aim: Chest

Action 1 onwards: Opponent is in full plate. Strat 1's trigger is true and remains true the entire fight. Strat 1 activates and stays active. Strategy 2 and 3 are never evaluated because the engine always uses the highest-priority strategy whose trigger is true. Warrior bashes aggressively at opponent's legs for the entire fight.

Why Strat 2 never fires: Even if the warrior takes 60% damage, Strat 2's trigger is ignored because Strat 1 (higher priority) is still true. The engine doesn't drop down to lower-priority strategies while a higher-priority trigger remains active.

Correct Scenario with Strat 2 firing: Strategy 1 is "When you've taken heavy damage" (heavy armor defensive mode), Strategy 2 is "Opponent is heavily armored" (heavy armor strategy). When you first take heavy damage, Strat 1 activates. While Strat 1's trigger remains true (you're still at heavy damage), it stays active. Once heavy damage threshold is no longer met, Strat 1's trigger becomes false, and the system checks Strat 2. If the opponent is still heavily armored, Strat 2 fires and governs the fight.

Your job: Design strategies with triggers that won't trap you. Order them carefully so higher-priority tactics fire when needed. Always have a "Always" fallback at the bottom.

Intimidation: The Presence Advantage

Warriors with high Presence (14+) can intimidate their opponents at fight start.

If you have high PRE and your opponent doesn't, there's a small chance your warrior intimidates them, causing them to lose initiative in the first minute. You get the upper hand from the opening bell.

This is rare but real. High-PRE warriors have an edge.

Concede Appeal: Mercy in Defeat

When a warrior is badly wounded (at or below 15% HP), they can beg for mercy.

This is a PRE-based roll. High-PRE warriors have better chances of receiving mercy (opponent concedes the fight). Low-PRE warriors rarely get it.

This is not something you control — it fires automatically if your warrior is dying. But it adds drama. Sometimes the underdog begs their way out.

The Champion System: Prestige Beyond Tiers

At the top of the rankings sits ONE champion — determined by the is_champion flag. The champion holds the highest honor in the arena and is featured in every newsletter.

Champion Selection Logic:
The Gladiatorial Commission uses the following rules to determine championship status:

  1. Direct Challenge: If a warrior defeats the sitting champion in combat, they immediately become the new champion
  2. Stripped Champion: If the Gladiatorial Commission strips a champion's title (for bullying), a new champion is selected from the recognition leader(s)
  3. Champion Sits Out: If the sitting champion doesn't fight this turn (or is promoted to Elite arena), the highest-recognition warrior becomes champion
  4. Champion Fought: If the sitting champion fought this turn and wasn't defeated, they retain the title

Tie-Breaker Cascade (when warriors are tied at highest recognition):
When selecting a new champion and multiple warriors are tied at the highest recognition level, the following cascade resolves the tie:

  1. Most Kills — warrior with the highest kill count
  2. Most Wins — warrior with the most victories
  3. Fewest Losses — warrior with the lowest loss count
  4. Highest Popularity — warrior with the best crowd favor rating
  5. Alphabetical by Name — final tie-breaker (guarantees a single champion)

Only warriors in the top 2 populated tiers of your arena can challenge the champion. You must climb high to take a shot at the crown.

The Bullying Penalty: If the champion blood-challenges a much weaker opponent (40+ recognition gap), they commit a "bullying offense." Each offense escalates the Gladiatorial Commission's chance to strip the title (15% → 32% → 49% → 65% per turn, capped at 65%).

This encourages champions to defend against worthy opponents, not prey on weaklings.

Advancement & Progression

Recognition: Your Warrior's Resume

Recognition (prestige score) is the number that determines your tier and your standing in the arena.

How You Gain Recognition:

  • Win a fight: +3 to +5 recognition
  • Beat a more-experienced opponent: bonus +1 to +3 (underdog bonus)
  • Crush an opponent decisively (health, speed, knockdowns): bonus +1 to +3
  • Kills: slightly higher gains than regular wins
  • High popularity: Warriors with many fans gain more per win

How You Lose Recognition:

  • Lose a fight: -3 recognition (minimum)
  • Lose to a weaker opponent: penalty -1 to -4 (underdog reversal)
  • Lose quickly or badly: larger losses
  • Go down fighting (show courage in defeat): +1 to +2 credit

Your recognition number slowly climbs as you win more than you lose. Tier progression is automatic — when you hit certain recognition thresholds, you advance tiers.

Training: The Long Grind

Warriors improve through training. Each turn, you can queue up to 3 training sessions per warrior.

Training Options:

You can train:

  • Skills (60 total): 16 non-weapon + 44 weapon-specific
  • Attributes (5 trainable): STR, DEX, CON, INT, PRE (SIZ is fixed forever)

Training Success:
Training is not guaranteed. Higher Intelligence (INT) increases success chance when training skills. Higher Constitution (CON) increases success chance when training attributes. Humans and Gnomes train slightly faster at both.

As warriors improve, training gets harder. A warrior with Dodge 0 trains easily. A warrior with Dodge 8 trains very slowly.

Time Investment:
A warrior early in their career can gain multiple skill levels per turn if lucky. Late-career warriors might gain 1 level per turn on high skills. Attribute training is slower overall.

Building a perfect warrior takes many turns (dozens to hundreds of fights).

The 16 Non-Weapon Skills

Combat Skills (11):

  • Dodge: Better defensive rolls, fewer hits taken
  • Parry: Active blade defense, block attacks
  • Throw: Ranged attack damage (used in Opportunity Throw style)
  • Charge: Polearm-based charging attacks (enhanced damage on charge)
  • Lunge: High knockdown chance if lands (devastating but risky)
  • Disarm: Ability to disarm opponent's weapon
  • Initiative: Act first in turn order, control fight pace (very valuable)
  • Feint: Confuse opponent's defense, open gaps
  • Brawl: Unarmed/fist damage (when weaponless)
  • Sweep: Low attacks, knocking legs out (certain weapons only)
  • Cleave: Large overhead attacks, damage over wide area

Utility Skills (5):

  • Bash: Hammer/mace focused, stun and knockdown potential
  • Acrobatics: Ground-fighting and agility
  • Riposte: Counter-attack after successful parry
  • Slash: Blade-focused wound opening
  • Strike: Strong precise hits

Each skill has meaningful combat impact. None are "dumps." A warrior with high DEX will have better initiative rolls and more attacks. A warrior with high Initiative will attack first in turn order. A warrior with high Dodge will avoid many hits. These matter.

Weapon Skills: One Per Weapon

There are 44 weapon skills (one for each weapon). Training a weapon skill increases accuracy and damage with that weapon specifically.

A warrior with Shortsword skill of 7 will hit and damage better with a shortsword than someone with Shortsword skill of 2. Weapon skills are crucial to combat effectiveness.

Attribute Training: The Slow Climb

Five attributes can be trained (SIZ cannot):

  • Strength (STR): Increases damage output and carrying capacity. Slow to train at high levels.
  • Dexterity (DEX): Increases attack accuracy and defense rolls. Provides bonuses to initiative rolls. Highly valuable for combat.
  • Constitution (CON): Increases HP and endurance. Helps warriors survive.
  • Intelligence (INT): Increases training success chance. Also helps with tactical advantage.
  • Presence (PRE): Increases recognition gains per win and intimidation chances. Slower direct combat benefit.

Training Attributes: Attributes train similarly to skills, but use Constitution (CON) to increase success chance instead of Intelligence (INT). Unlike skills (which cap at 9), attributes can be trained from their starting value up to 25. Training gets progressively harder after level 7-8. Reaching maximum (25) would take an extremely long time and dedication.

Popularity: The Crowd's Favor

As your warrior fights, the crowd's opinion changes. Exciting wins, come-from-behind victories, defeats of strong opponents — all increase popularity.

High-popularity warriors gain more recognition per win (the crowd's cheers boost morale and fame).

This is largely narrative flavor, but it has mechanical impact on prestige growth.

Injuries: The Permanent Scars

When your warrior is hit hard enough during a fight, they can suffer injuries to body locations: head, chest, abdomen, arms, or legs.

Injuries persist until the warrior dies. They don't heal. They accumulate over career.

Higher injury levels cause worse combat penalties. A warrior with 7+ injuries to a location is severely impacted. By contrast, level 3 injuries are annoying but manageable.

Injuries tell your warrior's story. That scar on the arm from the fight with the Half-Orc? It's still there, still slowing them down.

Elite Spire Exception: If your warrior is promoted to the Elite Spire, they stop accumulating new injuries (but keep the ones they had at promotion). See Advanced Arenas guide for details.

Special Events & Consequences

Death in the Arena

When your warrior falls to -HP (zero health or below), a death check triggers. Death is not immediate — instead, a cycle of death checks and concede appeals begins:

The Death Cycle:

  1. Warrior reaches -HP → Death check occurs (Presence-based concede appeal)
  2. If concede appeal succeeds → Mercy is granted, warrior survives this turn
  3. If concede appeal fails → Warrior continues fighting (not dead yet)
  4. If hit again while below -HP → Another death check occurs (failure rate increases)
  5. After this new damage → Another concede appeal attempt
  6. This cycle repeats: damage → death check (higher failure %) → concede appeal

The longer a warrior stays below -HP and takes additional damage, the higher the chance they fail a death check and are killed outright. Eventually, either:

  • A concede appeal succeeds (mercy granted)
  • A death check fails and they are slain

The Soul Spire Exception: If a warrior meets the Soul Spire criteria at the moment they are slain, they are transcended to the Soul Spire instead of dying permanently. This is rare — most warriors who fall in battle are truly gone. But warriors with an exceptional record may escape death and continue their legend on another plane. See the Advanced Arenas guide for details on Soul Spire transcendence criteria.

Slain warriors are archived. Their slot opens. You must create a replacement. Transcended warriors also leave a vacant slot (in the normal arena), but they continue fighting in the Soul Spire under your management.

That's it. They're gone. There is no resurrection in the normal arena. Dead is dead — unless they transcend to the Soul Spire.

This is intentional. Death must feel permanent. The finality of it creates drama.

Blood Challenges: Vengeance

When your warrior is killed, surviving teammates have the option to challenge the killer.

A blood challenge is auto-created and available for selection. The blood challenge remains active for 3 turns that the killer actually participates in fights. If the killing warrior sits out for 3 turns to avoid retribution, the blood challenge does not expire — it remains active until the killer fights and the 3-participation countdown begins.

Strategic Considerations:

  • Just because a blood challenge is available doesn't mean you should attempt it. Consider your remaining warriors' strength and experience.
  • If your best warrior was killed and your next-best is severely lacking experience or strength, attempting revenge might be too risky and could result in a second loss.
  • Strategic restraint is sometimes wiser than automatic vengeance.

Satisfying the Challenge:
Once you avenge your fallen warrior and win the blood challenge, it is satisfied and removed. This prevents the killer from repeatedly challenging your weakest warriors for easy wins — each blood challenge stands until fulfilled.

Winning a blood challenge feels satisfying and gives closure. Losing it compounds the tragedy by adding another warrior to your losses.

Retirement: A Peaceful End

After 50 fights, your warrior is eligible for retirement. You can request it via the want_retire flag.

When your warrior retires, they fight one final time that turn. After the turn completes, they hang up their weapons.

Retired warriors are archived as "Legacy" warriors. Their slot opens for replacement.

Retirement is a graceful exit. Your most-accomplished warrior gets to end on their terms, not dead in the arena.

Ascension to Monsterdom: The Ultimate Achievement

If your warrior defeats a Monster in combat (an ultra-rare achievement), they are absorbed into The Monsters.

Your warrior becomes a permanent part of the Monster difficulty curve. They are no longer manageable. They will fight on the Monster roster as NPC-controlled creatures.

This is both an honor and a loss. Your warrior achieved the ultimate victory, but you can no longer control them.

The Elite Spire Exception: If a warrior kills a Monster on their final turn before being promoted to the Elite Spire arena, an extraordinary event occurs. The warrior is promoted to the Elite Spire as planned, and you retain full control of them there. However, the Gods of the arena will clone your warrior to create a permanent Monster roster version. You will manage the Elite Spire warrior, while the cloned Monster version is controlled by the game engine. This is the rarest of all achievements — ascending to the Elite arena while also becoming legend.

This is permanent. The warrior is gone from your roster (except in Elite arena), transformed into a legend.

Quick Reference & Tips

Early-Game Advice

Starting Out:

  • Build balanced warriors (don't spike one stat too high)
  • Focus on dodge/parry early for survival
  • Weapon skills come second
  • Attribute training comes later (gets progressively harder)
  • Don't worry about perfect strategies; basic ones work fine

Gear Selection:

  • Light armor = faster actions, less defense
  • Heavy armor = fewer actions, more defense
  • Match gear to your strategy (aggressive wants light, defensive wants heavy)
  • Don't over-equip; STR matters

Strategy Design Tips

Always Have a Fallback:
Make sure one of your strategies is "Always" (typically your lowest-priority slot) to catch unmatched triggers and ensure your warrior always has a strategy to follow.

Tier Strategies by Scenario:

  • Light armor → Pierce and penetrate
  • Heavy armor → Bash and stun
  • Weaponless opponent → Aggressive press
  • You're hurt → Defend

Activity Level Matters:

  • Level 0-2 = Conservation mode (low endurance drain)
  • Level 5 = Balanced (default)
  • Level 9 = Berserker (high endurance drain, many actions)

Aim/Defense Points:

  • Protect injured locations
  • Exploit opponent weaknesses
  • Target specific body parts for tactical advantage

Test and Iterate:
You'll learn through trial-and-error. Watch how your warriors perform. Adjust strategies that aren't working.

Common Questions

How do I increase HP?

CON training (direct), STR training (secondary), certain races (racial HP bonus). Also, level up Constitution to maximize health pool.

How do I hit more often?

High DEX, high Initiative skill, weapons with fast attack rates, activity level 7-9 in strategy.

How do I defend better?

High DEX, trained Dodge/Parry, armor/helms, activity level 1-3 (conservative), defensive fighting styles.

How do I become champion?

Defeat the sitting champion in battle. Champion status is determined by the is_champion flag, not by tier or recognition score. You can become champion at any recognition level or tier by defeating the current title holder.

Why did my warrior lose when they had better stats?

Luck (random 1-30 value per warrior), strategy matchup (bad strategy vs opponent), injuries (cumulative penalties), or simply outplayed. Stats are not destiny.

Can I change my strategy during a fight?

No, you can't. But your strategy automatically adapts based on triggers — once a trigger condition is met, the strategy persists and executes until overridden by a higher-priority trigger. That IS strategy mid-fight.

What if I don't like how my warrior turned out?

Let them die and create a replacement. This is the only reset. Choices are permanent.

Can I have duplicate warriors?

No. Each warrior is unique. You cannot create two warriors with the same name or stats. Names are unique identifiers.

How long does it take to develop a "good" warrior?

Depends entirely on win rate. A warrior on a hot streak can climb tiers quickly. A struggling warrior advances slowly. Early tiers can be reached in 10-20 fights if winning consistently. Elite tier might take 50-100+ fights depending on skill and luck. There's no fixed timeline — it's determined by how often your warrior wins.

Advanced Strategy Concepts

The Initiative Advantage:
High DEX warriors get better bonuses to initiative rolls. Better initiative rolls mean more chances to act first in each round, which gives more opportunities to attack and control the pace of combat. DEX is one of the most valuable stats for combat effectiveness.

The Endurance Attrition:
Long fights favor warriors with high CON and balanced activity levels. Berserker warriors (activity 9) burn out quickly. Conservative warriors (activity 0) can outlast in 20+ minute bouts.

The Injury Management:
Accumulate injuries strategically. Use injuries to identify weakness and protect vulnerable areas in strategy.

The Underdog Bonus:
A rookie who defeats an expert gets a massive recognition boost. Use this to climb fast — challenge high tiers and hope for upset wins.

Detailed FAQ

Game Mechanics

Can I control my warrior's moves during a fight?

No. Your warrior follows pre-programmed strategies. You set the strategy; the combat engine executes it.

Can I have 6+ warriors on my roster?

No. Exactly 5 warriors per team, always. When one dies, you replace them. However, you can manage up to 5 teams, so up to 25 warriors total across all your teams.

How long is a fight?

Most fights complete in under 5 minutes. After 7 minutes, the referee begins to intervene to speed up the process. Fights continue until a warrior falls to exhaustion, dies, or concedes.

Can a fight end in a draw?

No. One warrior always wins. Fights are decided by knockout (exhaustion/death) or successful concede appeal.

Do warriors learn from fights?

No. Improvements come only from training (queued skill/attribute advancement).

How often can I upload?

As many times as you need during the OPEN phase. Changes are only locked in when the turn RUNS (PROCESSING phase begins). Re-upload to make changes and adjustments until you're satisfied.

Warriors & Advancement

Can I edit a warrior after they're created?

No. Warriors are locked at creation. You can only change equipment and strategies and training queues. Base stats, race, gender, name are permanent.

How do I increase a warrior's attributes?

Train them. Queue STR/DEX/CON/INT/PRE in the training queue. Success is probabilistic and harder as the attribute rises.

Can Size be trained?

No. Size is fixed at creation forever.

How fast do warriors improve?

Depends on their INT and luck. Early on (low skill levels), fast. Late career (high skill levels), slow.

Can I reset a warrior's progress?

No. Only way to start over is let them die and create a new warrior.

Combat & Matchmaking

Why does my warrior fight the same opponent repeatedly?

Matchmaking avoids rematches from the last 3 turns, but not beyond. If your tier is small, repeated matchups are common.

Can I choose my opponent in standard matches?

No. Matchmaking pairs you automatically. Use challenges if you want to select opponents.

How much does luck matter?

Significantly. Luck (1-30, assigned at creation) affects every dice roll. Lucky warriors outperform stats; unlucky underperform.

Can I see my warrior's luck?

No. It's hidden. You'll discover it through your warrior's fight record over time.

Do strategy triggers fire reliably?

Yes. They're deterministic (not random). If trigger condition is met, strategy fires.

Death & Consequences

What happens when my warrior dies?

They're archived. Their slot opens. You must create a replacement.

Can dead warriors fight again?

No. Death is permanent. You must create a new warrior.

Can I save a warrior from death?

Only if they successfully appeal for mercy (PRE-based, automatic). Otherwise, slain warriors are gone.

What is a blood challenge?

Vengeance fight auto-created when your warrior is killed. Available for 3 turns that the killer participates (sitting out doesn't expire the challenge). It's your choice whether to attempt it. Once you win a blood challenge, it's satisfied and removed. You must decide if your remaining warriors are strong enough to win. Not every blood challenge should be pursued.

Advanced Arenas

What are the Elite Spire and Soul Spire?

Advanced arenas for elite warriors and transcended souls. See "Advanced Arenas" guide for details.

Can normal warriors join advanced arenas?

Yes, through promotion (Elite Spire) or transcendence on death (Soul Spire). Details in Advanced Arenas guide.

Is there a way to prevent a warrior from going to the advanced arenas?

No. If they qualify, they will be called. It's an honor and a promotion.
